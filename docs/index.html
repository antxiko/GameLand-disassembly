Game Land is not a game: it is a cartridge for making games without programming. You pick the kind (a shooting game or a maze) and the background, draw it with LINE, BOX, CIRCLE and PAINT, make the characters dot by dot or from its 20 samples, compose the tunes on a staff, decide how each one moves and play it; then you save it to tape. Here it is, all of it, from the inside: 26,084 bytes of code, with an engine that plays what you make and its own drawing library.

Casio - (c) 1984 - 32 KB cartridge (header at 0x4000) - MSX (the Japanese font: Casio PV-16) - Dump 39ffaacd...

There are no screenshots here. The GIFs are the ROM running in openMSX (a Casio PV-16) with a script taken from the listing: on every frame the VRAM, the RAM and the VDP registers are logged, and our own code paints them. The samples come from the bytes of the ROM.

What is inside

The game

  • The program: the intro, the menus (in Japanese and translated), the keys and the kinds of game.
  • The editors: a background drawn with its tools, characters from the samples, the sounds, the story and the movements.
  • The music: the four sample tunes, from its own sound engine.
  • The sprites: the program's own sprites, its cursors and the clefs, drawn by running its own routine.

Curiosities

  • Curiosities: a second cartridge, a tablet, code that writes code, randomness read from the ROM, dead code.
  • Secrets and bugs: what was searched for: HOME and the second cartridge, the tablet, the keyboard, the score of the games that goes back to zero past 999,990, and the extra lives checked.

Under the hood

  • Under the hood: rebuilding the ROM, the cartridge, the game it stores, the engine that plays it, the drawing library, the tape and the RAM.
